# Public Communications Inventory — L&S11 & F&R11

Compiled 2026-09-18 · links verified 2026-09-21

## Scope and method

This inventory lists written and verbal public communications by the four authors of two pilot papers, within a fixed window around each publication: three months before to one year after.

| Paper | Publication | Window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loehle C. & Scafetta N. (2011), "Climate Change Attribution Using Empirical Decomposition of Climatic Data," *Open Atmospheric Science Journal* 5:74–86 ("L&S11") | late July 2011 (launch posts of 2011-07-25/26 describe it as just published) | ≈ 2011-04-25 → 2012-07-25 |
| Foster G. & Rahmstorf S. (2011), "Global temperature evolution 1979–2010," *Environmental Research Letters* 6, 044022 ("F&R11") | 2011-12-06 | 2011-09-06 → 2012-12-06 |

Inclusion: communications by an author in public (non-scholarly) media that discuss the paper's findings or conclusions. Direct author interview or press-release quotes count; journalist quotes lifted from the paper text do not. Journals, arXiv and scientific conferences are excluded by design.

Compiled 2026-09-18 from two research passes; every link individually re-verified 2026-09-21 (statuses in the tables and under Verification status). Pass 1 was unrestricted; pass 2 was English-only with broadcast media (radio, TV, podcasts, recorded talks) included. Two German-language pass-1 items are retained and tagged \[DE\]. Tables run oldest to newest so each rollout reads in sequence.

## Institutional and shared items

Both papers had an institutional layer, of structurally different kinds: advocacy-platform infrastructure for L&S11, publisher and research-institute press machinery for F&R11.

- **L&S11.** WUWT hosted the full paper PDF and supplementary information on its own file server from launch day ([archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20220412003236/http://wattsupwiththat.files.wordpress.com/2011/07/loehlescafetta.pdf) — Wayback snapshot of 2022-04-12, verified via the availability API on 2026-09-21; the original wattsupwiththat.files.wordpress.com URL died with WUWT's move off WordPress.com hosting). Heartland conference platforms carried the verbal side: Scafetta at ICCC-6 pre-publication, Loehle at ICCC-7 in-window. SPPI carried a reprint of the companion harmonic-model material that Scafetta linked from his WUWT posts.
- **F&R11.** The Institute of Physics issued a press release on 2011-12-05, ["Global warming 'not slowing down,' say researchers"](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/12/111205191724.htm), with an author quote calling the pause idea a "groundless misconception" (the syndicated text does not attribute the quote to one author). A second coordinated IOP-plus-PIK release round on 2012-11-28 accompanied the follow-up paper (see the Rahmstorf table). An ERL highlights or best-article recognition was not verified in either pass; no claim is made.

## Broadcast sweep: findings and nulls

No in-window English radio, TV or podcast interviews were found for any of the four authors; the nearest equivalents are recorded think-tank conference talks on the contrarian side.

- **Scafetta**: ICCC-6 talk (2011-06/07), video archived by Heartland — the one verified in-window recorded verbal item tied to the paper's claims.
- **Loehle**: ICCC-7 talk (2012-05), video page was hosted by Heartland but is now a 404 with no Wayback snapshot found; topic unverified.
- **Foster**: no broadcast presence identified at all; blog-only.
- **Rahmstorf**: substantial 2011 broadcast activity exists but is German (ARD Morgenmagazin, Deutschlandfunk, RBB). The English-only restriction removes essentially his whole broadcast footprint — flag this in any cross-paper comparison so the null is not misread as inactivity.

Net effect of the language filter: it is roughly neutral for Loehle, Scafetta and Foster, but asymmetrically suppresses Rahmstorf's record.

## Appendix: post-window fate of the L&S11 forecast test (2012–2023)

The WUWT widget was the paper's self-declared public test — "a long term public test to determine if it is right" (Watts, launch thread) and "will the forecast hold, or my proposed model is just another failed attempt…? Time will tell" (Scafetta, on the page itself). No statement by Loehle or Scafetta acknowledging the forecast's failure was found in this sweep. Scafetta's later publications claim vindication on stale or favorable series, using the ENSO exemption installed on the 2012 page; Loehle never publicly revisited the forecast after his April 2012 Mann rebuttal. Within the skeptic orbit, the only negative judgments were contemporaneous — [Eschenbach's July 2011 critique](https://wattsupwiththat.com/2011/07/30/riding-a-pseudocycle/) and launch-thread commenters ("It looks to me like the prediction has already failed") — and the nearest institutional verdict is the silent deletion of the test page. Post-hoc failure assessments came only from critics: the SkS thread above, HotWhopper (2016), and the replication literature ([Benestad et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00704-015-1597-5), in the project files).

Coding note (post-publication conduct): a self-declared public test abandoned after one update cycle and deleted without verdict; subsequent verification claims made on discontinued or favorable datasets; disconfirming intervals reclassified as out of scope.
