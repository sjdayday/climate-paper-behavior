### 1.3 Census or capped sample? (D3 question)

A capped sample of at most 10 replies per author per thread, simulated 1,000 times (seed 20260926) on this census:

| Quantity | Census (313 replies) | Capped (94 replies): mean [5 %, 95 %] |
|---|---|---|
| Scafetta, person-directed register per 1,000 words | 1.46 | 1.48 [0.73, 2.29] |
| Loehle, same | 0.85 | 0.53 [0.00, 1.51] |
| Foster, same | 4.62 | 6.02 [3.65, 9.27] |
| Rahmstorf, same | 1.03 | 0.91 [0.00, 1.59] |
| Share of claim-bearing replies retained | 1.00 | 0.30 [0.19, 0.41] |
| Share of the 14 dyads (≥ 3 exchanges) left intact | 1.00 | **0.01 [0.00, 0.07]** |

**What changes if all replies are scored.**
1. **Claim scoring:** the cap discards about 70 % of the replies that carry paper claims. They cluster in the first days and in a few long replies, so they cannot be recovered by sampling more cleverly.
2. **Register rates:** these are roughly unbiased under the cap but two to three times noisier, and a single long reply can dominate (Foster's final reply to one commenter holds 6 of his 32 hits).
3. **Dyad analysis:** it only works on a census. A cap keeps an intact reply sequence for almost no dyad, so the "register is personal, built over time" hypothesis (§2) becomes untestable.
4. **Weighting:** a cap equalizes authors mechanically. A census does the same through author-cluster averaging without discarding data.
5. **Cost:** the register pass is mechanical. Claim scoring runs only on keyword-flagged candidate sentences (321 here, 38 scored), so it grows with the number of replies but stays small.
6. **Retrieval completeness is the binding constraint,** not scoring cost. Threads must be captured from archives when live sites paginate, and completeness must be reported per thread.

**Recommendation: census,** with author-cluster aggregation and dyad identifiers (adopted in protocol v0.2 §3.4).

### 2.1 Is register personal and cumulative? (dyads)

Dyads are author→addressee pairs with ≥ 3 exchanges; 14 exist in the census.
- Replies **inside** these dyads carry **2.43 person-directed hits per 1,000 words**, against **0.96** in other replies, a ratio of about 2.5.
- Within dyads, escalation is **not uniform**:

| Dyad | Exchanges | Hits per exchange, in order | Span |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scafetta → Svalgaard | 36 | 0 ×11, 1, 0, 2, 0, 0, 1, 1, …, 3, 1, 1, …, 1, 1 | 2011-07 → 2012-03 (3 threads) |
| Scafetta → Eschenbach | 32 | early cluster (6 of the first 11), then sparse | 2011-07 → 2012-03 |
| Scafetta → Vukcevic | 9 | 0, 0, 0, 0, 2, 0, 0, 0, 1 | 2011-07 → 2012-03 |
| Foster → Chris Carthew | 8 | 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 0, 2, **6** | 2011-12-17 → 12-25 |
| Foster → Colin Aldridge | 6 | 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1 | 2011-12 |

This supports the interpretation that register is partly **relational**: it concentrates in repeated exchanges with specific interlocutors, sometimes across months and threads (Scafetta–Svalgaard). It does not show monotone escalation within a dyad. The protocol therefore records dyad identity, exchange index, and cross-thread history (v0.2 PC-9b), so the later "evolution of the discussion" analysis can separate three things:
- **era effects** (the discussion as a whole);
- **author effects** (a person's baseline register);
- **dyad effects** (history with a particular opponent).

The pilot cannot say anything about change over time yet. It has two eras (2011–12, 2021) with different authors and genres. The one author present in both, Scafetta, is 0.94 per 1,000 words in his 2012 posts and 0 in his 169-word 2021 quote.

## 5. Stage 1 note (for Steve's decision)

RF9 exposes a question about Exhibit 2. There, F&R11 P3 = 1 rested on input substitutions (SOI, Ammann AOD, sunspots) and leave-one-out checks whose results the paper **states but does not show**: "None of these substitutions affected the results in a significant way". Under codebook v0.2 §6, a textual assertion that an unreported alternative gave similar results **does not qualify**; this is the same rule that corrected LS11's P3 upward, because LS11's results *were* shown. P3 may still be 1 on a different basis: results are shown for five alternative temperature datasets (Table 1). This is logged as a Stage 1 erratum candidate. The score is not changed here, because Stage 2 cannot revise Stage 1 (protocol §2.2).
