### 1b. Tier-basis ledger

Each entry: **claim level → tier · O**, then the **body location**, a **verbatim supporting phrase**, and **why that yields the tier**. For FR11 the recurring basis is T3 by *multiple independent datasets* (five temperature records) and/or *formal stated-error uncertainty* (autocorrelation-corrected standard errors).

**F1 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3 (Results) and Table 1. Supporting phrase: p3 — "All five records show highly statistically significant warming, at rates ranging from 0.014 K yr-1 (UAH) to 0.018 K yr-1 (GISS)"; p3 — "Standard errors for the trend rates take into account the autocorrelation of the residuals." Why T3: five independent temperature records converge **and** each trend carries a formally corrected standard error (Table 1) — both T3 routes satisfied at once. L3 permitted at T3 → O = 0.

**F2 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Table 1 probable errors; Fig. 4. Supporting phrase: p3 — "the adjusted data sets show noticeably less short-term variation than the raw data … the five records reflect the same essential changes." Why T3: the noise-reduction claim is demonstrated across all five records (Fig. 4) with the reduction in probable errors quantified in Table 1 — multi-dataset convergence + stated uncertainty. O = 0.

**F3 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Fig. 3. Supporting phrase: p3 — "The coefficients of temperature response to MEI, AOD and TSI are plotted in figure 3, with 2-sigma error bars. The LT records show a markedly stronger response … than … the surface records." Why T3: the LT-vs-surface contrast is read off regression coefficients across the five datasets with 2-σ error bars — independent sources + formal uncertainty. O = 0.

**F4 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3 (Table 1) and p5 (§4). Supporting phrases: p3 — adjusted-vs-raw trends with reduced probable errors (Table 1); p5 — "the estimated warming rate does not show any sign of decrease, no matter what the starting year, within the estimated (2-sigma) uncertainty." Why T3: "steady" is supported by three convergent in-paper analyses — the trend table, the all-start-years scan (Fig. 6), and the quadratic test (p5) — each with stated 2-σ uncertainty. Multiple methods + formal error → T3. O = 0.

**F5 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Fig. 5. Supporting phrase: p3 — "for all five adjusted data sets, 2009 and 2010 are the two hottest years on record." Why T3: a descriptive ranking, but asserted only where it holds across all five independent adjusted records (Fig. 5 annual averages) → multi-dataset convergence (T3). L3 at T3 → O = 0.

**F6 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Figs. 3–4. Supporting phrase: p3 — "The coefficients of temperature response … are plotted in figure 3, with 2-sigma error bars." Why T3: "confirms the strong influence of known factors" rests on the regression coefficients across five datasets with formal error bars, plus the variance reduction visible in Fig. 4. O = 0.

**F7 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Fig. 3. Supporting phrase: p3 — "The LT records show a markedly stronger response to both the MEI and to AOD than do the surface records." Why T3: identical basis to F3 (Fig. 3 coefficients across datasets, 2-σ). O = 0.

**F8 · L3 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p3; Fig. 4. Supporting phrase: p3 — "When the estimated influence of the exogenous factors is removed … the adjusted data sets show noticeably less short-term variation." Why T3: the removal/isolation is demonstrated across five records (Fig. 4). **Register note (logged, not scored):** "isolating" asserts a complete separation the regression only approximates; inside the letter of L3 at T3, routed to S2 for capture. O = 0.

**F9 · L4 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p5 (§4). Supporting phrases: p5 — "the estimated warming rate does not show any sign of decrease, no matter what the starting year, within the … (2-sigma) uncertainty"; p5 — the quadratic test, significant only for UAH (p = 0.03) and "regard[ed] with skepticism because it shows in no other data set." Why T3: the "remarkably steady" claim is backed by the start-year scan (Fig. 6) **and** the quadratic test across five datasets, with formal uncertainty and an explicit multiplicity caution. The booster "clearly" (L4) is permitted at T3 → O = 0 (booster routed to S2).

**F10 · L4 → T3 · O = 0.** Body: p5 (§4). Supporting phrase: p5 — "the estimated warming rate does not show any sign of decrease, no matter what the starting year, within the estimated (2-sigma) uncertainty." Why T3: the unhedged negative-universal "no indication of any slowdown or acceleration" is backed by two convergent in-paper tests (all-start-years scan Fig. 6; quadratic test) with stated 2-σ uncertainty across five datasets. L4 at T3 → O = 0.
> **Cross-paper audit anchor.** F10 and LS11's A3 / C7 carry the **same L4 negative-universal marker** ("no … any" / "not able to"). The *only* thing that differs is the tier: F10 is T3 (two in-paper tests + formal uncertainty, five datasets) → O = 0; LS11 A3 is T1 (single in-sample residual) → O = 2; LS11 C7 is T0 (citation-only, no in-paper GCM analysis) → O = 3. ## 2. Index E — Contextual engagement

**E1 = 2 (engaged), under the aligned-paper rule (codebook §5, r6).** For a paper whose central claim aligns with the contemporaneous assessment, the counter-position is the most prominent contemporaneous published challenge — here, that warming had stopped or that the records disagree. The paper names it — "debate has arisen over discrepancies between the different global temperature records, and over … possible recent changes in, warming rates" (p1) — and engages it directly (five-record comparison; the all-start-years trend analysis of Fig. 6 addresses start-year selection head-on).
**E1b = 0.** The engagement's load-bearing evidence is the paper's own new analysis plus non-self citations (Peterson & Baringer 2009); no author self-citations exist in the paper.
**E2 = 17/19 = 0.895.** Reference list (pp. 7–8) totals 19 entries. Non-journal (2): IPCC 2007 (assessment report); Wolter & Timlin 1993 (workshop proceedings). Remaining 17 journal articles. (Dataset URLs appear as footnotes, not references.)
**E3 = 0/19 = 0.000.** No reference shares an author with the paper.
**E4 = 1.** Benchmark-corpus candidates cited: IPCC 2007; Lean & Rind 2008.
**ENG = (1.000 + 0.895 + 1.000 + 1.000)/4 = 0.974.**

## 7. Judgment log and candidate amendments arising from this exhibit

1. **r6 (E1 operationalization gap).** The v0.1 counter-position rule presumed a paper opposing the IPCC assessment and was undefined for assessment-aligned papers — an asymmetry in the instrument itself, caught only because a mainstream paper was piloted. Fixed for freeze (now codebook §5).
2. **P1a = 0 is the pilot's sharpest cross-paper demonstration that the instrument follows its rules.** The mainstream paper fails both out-of-sample items that the contrarian paper partially passes (LS11 P1a = 1). FR11's design contains no held-out evaluation; robustness to input substitution (P3) is not out-of-sample support and was not allowed to masquerade as such.
3. **F11/F14 forecast flags:** the forward-looking statements carry no uncertainty intervals — the same flag LS11's forecasts received. Flag count symmetric across the pair (2–2), as is the normative-flag count (1–1).
4. **Tier-basis expansion (this edition).** The ledger (§1b) makes FR11's zeros auditable: all 14 claims rest on T3-grade support (five-record convergence and/or autocorrelation-corrected uncertainty), so even the four L4 boosters (F9, F10, F13, F14) sit at or below their permitted ceiling. The decisive audit anchor is **F10 vs LS11 A3/C7** (same L4 negative-universal marker, tiers T3/T1/T0, O = 0/2/3) — the pilot pair's single clearest proof that the instrument scores warranted confidence rather than surface rhetoric. One latent inconsistency in S3's recorded figure/equation ratio is carried forward unchanged and flagged for the eventual S-index pass; it does not affect Index C.
5. **A-index blind spot** (§4 narrative note): conduct-under-criticism items cannot register self-initiated re-testing (Rahmstorf, Foster & Cahill 2017); logged as a design observation, not an amendment proposal.
