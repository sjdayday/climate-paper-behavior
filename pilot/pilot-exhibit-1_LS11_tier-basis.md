All Index E / P / A / S scores, judgment-log items, and composites are carried forward unchanged (with the erratum's P3 correction folded in).

A note on what a tier is, for the auditor (codebook §4.3): the tier records **the strongest support the paper itself provides for that specific claim** — T0 asserted or citation-only; T1 in-sample fit only; T2 out-of-sample support *that the paper frames and evaluates as a test*; T3 multiple independent in-paper data sources/methods converging, or a formal stated-error model bounding the claim. Permitted maximum certainty by tier (§4.4): T0→L1, T1→L2, T2→L3, T3→L4. Overreach **O = max(0, level − permitted)**. The claim quote (what the author wrote) lives in the scoring table; the **tier-basis quote (where the paper backs it up)** lives in the ledger — they come from different parts of the paper, which is the whole point of the check.

### 1b. Tier-basis ledger

Each entry opens with the **ID · level → tier · O**, then restates the scored **claim** (labeled *Claim:*) so the entry stands alone without scrolling back to the table, and then gives the **body location**, a **verbatim supporting phrase**, and **why that yields the tier**. The restated *Claim* is the author's sentence being scored; the "supporting phrase" is the paper's own justifying discussion, located away from that sentence — the two come from different parts of the paper, which is exactly the comparison the check exists to enable.

**A1 · L3 → T2 · O = 0.** *Claim:* "the residuals showed an approximate linear upward trend after 1942" Body: pp. 6–8. The residual trend is a property of the model calibrated on pre-1950 data and extrapolated past 1950 — which the paper explicitly frames as a predictive test. Supporting phrases: p6 — "fit the model to the period prior to elevated greenhouse gases and then extrapolate it forward … the model would be tested on its forecasting capability"; p8 — "slope of 0.66 +/- 0.08ºC/century (R2 = 0.59) and begins (>0) in 1942." Why T2: the pre-1950 calibration + post-1950 extrapolation is framed and evaluated as a test (§4.3 T2), and the trend is measured on that held-out interval. L3 is permitted at T2 → O = 0. *(This is the paper's one genuine out-of-sample move; it earns the T2 and the calibrated score.)*

**A2 · L3 → T1 · O = 1.** *Claim:* "The resulting full natural plus anthropogenic model fits the entire 160 year record very well." Body: p9. Supporting phrase: p9 — "This model (Fig. 3a) fits the entire 160-year record better than any model we have seen." Why T1: the anthropogenic linear term is fit on the 1950–2010 residuals and then added back; the "fits very well" judgment is then made on the same full record that includes those data — in-sample fit by construction (§4.3 T1). T1 permits L2; the unhedged "fits … very well" is L3 → O = 1.

**A3 · L4 ⟦E2⟧ → T1 · O = 2.** *Claim:* "Residual analysis does not provide any evidence for a substantial cooling effect due to sulfate aerosols from 1940 to 1970." Body: p13. Supporting phrases: p13 — "the residuals from the pre-1950 model … 1950s through 1970s show not a decline but a constant linear rise"; p13 — "Our full model … shows a cooling in the 1950s through 1970s without any forcing from tropospheric aerosols (Fig. 3a)." Why T1: the no-aerosol-signal claim rests on the behavior of the paper's own in-sample residuals/full model; alternative decompositions that *could* carry an aerosol signal are not excluded, and no independent source is brought → in-sample only (T1). The unhedged negative-universal "does not provide any evidence" is L4 (§4.2(iii), r16c) → O = 4−2 = 2. *(Contrast C12 below, the same finding stated with a hedge.)*
