## 1. Derivation of the feature families

**1.1 Derivation principle.** The hypothesis is a claim about intended audience. Audiences differ in two ways that matter for instrument design: what they can **check**, and what they **reward**. A feature family is admitted when (i) peer-optimization and lay-optimization predict **divergent optima** on it, and (ii) the divergence is **observable** from the paper and the indexed public record without adjudicating scientific correctness. The admitted families tile the paper's life cycle — in the text (C, S), in relation to the field (E), before submission (P), after publication (A):

- **C — Calibration.** Peers re-derive whether stated confidence matches evidential warrant; lay readers consume confidence directly as a credibility cue. Decoupling confidence from tier is cheap and effective for one audience, costly before the other.
- **E — Engagement.** Peers know what was omitted; lay readers do not. Ignoring the counter-position is nearly free under lay-optimization.
- **P — Practices.** Uncertainty propagation, degrees-of-freedom discipline, and out-of-sample tests are costly signals legible mainly to experts — effort with little lay payoff.
- **A — Post-publication conduct.** The peer game continues after publication (indexed critique, replies, corrections); the lay-persuasion game largely ends at uptake.
- **S — Stylometry.** The direct textual fingerprint of the intended reader: readability, boosters, self-mention, figure/equation balance.

Structurally, the set is three constructs, each operationalized twice, plus one: C and S measure audience-tuned confidence at two grains (claim-level judgment; document-level mechanics — deliberate redundancy for triangulation); E and A measure relation to the critical community at two time points; P stands alone. The derivation is a sufficiency argument, not a uniqueness proof: other decompositions exist. These five also survived the study's auditability requirement (§1, §3) — each admits page-anchored, quote-bounded scoring or pure mechanical computation.

**1.2 Admission filters.** Candidate families were screened against five filters: **F1** no correctness adjudication; **F2** the paper's or authors' own conduct — never their identity, and never the ecosystem's reception of the work; **F3** anchorable — scorer judgment bounded and verifiable under §3 conventions; **F4** recoverable from the artifact plus indexed record across the full sampling window; **F5** era/venue robustness, or explicit era-conditioning.

**1.3 Families considered and excluded.**

| Family considered | Failed | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Error/correctness counting (replication-based) | F1 | The founding exclusion; the approach of [1]. Collapses into the object-level dispute; differential-misclassification risk (§1, Appendix B). |
| Author identity: credentials, affiliation, funding sources, COI content | F2 | Unit is the paper; identity features import ad hominem inference; ecosystem-level identity effects are the terrain of [2]. Funding-*disclosure presence* as a P-item also fails F5 (disclosure mandates vary by venue and era). |
| Venue features (journal rank, review rigor) | F2 + endogeneity | Venue is partly an *outcome* of the behavior under study (gatekeeping selects on it); recorded as covariate only (§2), never matched. |
| Reception/uptake (media coverage, citations received, altmetrics, think-tank adoption) | F2 | The ecosystem's behavior toward the paper, not the authors'. Boundary rule: authors' own post-publication conduct is in (Index A); others' response is out, except A1, scored only as the trigger condition for A2. |
| Graphical rhetoric (axis truncation, baseline selection, smoothing choices) | F3 → F1 | Every drafted item collapsed into "is this the *right* baseline?" — i.e., back into F1. S3 is retained as its mechanical shadow. Flagged for post-pilot revisit if anchorable. |
| Argumentation structure (Toulmin coding, argument mining) | F3/F4 | Historically poor inter-coder reliability at whole-paper grain; automated extraction unvalidated for this use. |
| Press-release/outreach behavior | F4, partly F2 | Unrecoverable for older sample papers; often institutional rather than author conduct. Model if ever recoverable: [7]. |
| "Mathiness"/obscurantism as a judgment family | folded into S3 | [19] shows added mathematics can *raise* non-expert quality ratings — one reason Index S directionality is exploratory, not confirmatory. |

**1.4 Precedent literature.** Each family has an established literature in at least one domain; the per-family nearest neighbors are:

- **C:** hedging/booster corpus linguistics [3, 4] — the lexicon source pinned for §4.2 and S1/S2 (Appendix C.1); biomedical "spin" coding of claim strength against evidential warrant [5, 6]; exaggeration relative to source evidence in press releases [7]; booster inflation over time [8]. Qualitative climate precedent for mainstream *under*-claiming ("erring on the side of least drama") [9] — one motivation for scoring CAL symmetrically in both groups.
- **E:** citation-function classification [10, 11]; citation-distortion network analysis [12]. Closest precedents anywhere: reference-corpus and in-text citation comparisons of IPCC vs. NIPCC assessment reports [13, 14, 15], including four-way coding of each citation's function relative to mainstream claims (supporting / not supporting / uncertainty-creating / neutral) [15].
- **P:** reporting-guideline adherence audits (CONSORT/PRISMA family); transparency-indicator metascience [16, 17]; automated statistical-consistency checking [18]. Within climate, [1] is precedent *and foil*: it replicates errors; Index P is the de-contested redesign, scoring practice presence instead of adjudicating mistakes.
- **A:** thinnest precedent as a scored index. Nearest neighbor [20]: a 330-journal audit finding 1.9% of sampled articles drew any indexed post-publication critique, with 44 of 58 critiques receiving author replies, 41 of which asserted the original conclusions were unchanged. Those base rates underwrite A2's discriminating power and identify A3's open boundary case ("analysis modified, conclusions maintained") as the modal reply posture across science.
- **S:** the stylometric tradition; readability drift over time [21]. Climate-direct precedent [22]: computational text analysis of IPCC AR5 WG1 vs. NIPCC CCR-II finding the IPCC authors used *more* cautious, tentative language — the direction H1 predicts — with explicit abstention from adjudicating either report's accuracy. Think-tank doubt-corpus text mining [23].

Common limitation of the climate precedents [13–15, 22]: the unit is the assessment report — **N = 2 corpora**, the pseudoreplication this study's author-clustered, matched journal-article design (§2, §13) is built to escape. The biomedical literatures have the sample sizes but score quality variance *within* the mainstream, not a contrarian/mainstream contrast. The useful contrast class is CARDS [24], which classifies *what* contrarian claims assert; this instrument scores *how papers warrant* claims — orthogonal constructs. To the authors' knowledge (as of 2026-11-14), no study combines all five families at the journal-article level on an author-clustered matched sample; a systematic scoping search confirming this is a pre-registration deliverable, **pending**.

## 2. The hedging/booster operationalization: precedents, alternatives, consensus

**2.1 Why this question matters.** Codebook Appendix C.1 pins "the Hyland lexicon" as the authority that supersedes §4.2's provisional booster enumeration and feeds S1/S2 at freeze. That pin needs a defense a reviewer will demand: which list exactly, and why a lexicon at all. The finance literature supplies the sharpest version of the stakes: [36] built domain-specific word lists (including strong/weak-modal categories) for corporate filings after showing that general-purpose dictionaries badly misclassify domain text — lexicon validity is domain-relative, so an unpinned "Hyland lexicon" is not yet an instrument.

**2.2 Uses of the approach across domains.** The hedge/booster framework is the standard instrument in its home field, applied linguistics / English for Academic Purposes: its canonical article-form statements are [25] (the stance-and-engagement model, whose inventories are the most-reused lists) and [26], built on corpora of 240 research articles across eight disciplines [3], with a large replication literature across disciplines and languages. Beyond the home field: diachronic metascience — [27] tracked stance-marker frequencies in research articles over roughly fifty years; medical discourse — [28] is a parallel hedging taxonomy for medical English predating [3]; climate — [22] measured the same construct on IPCC vs. NIPCC reports but with general-purpose text analyzers (LIWC-type certainty/tentativeness categories [37]), *not* Hyland's lists, a precision this study's precedent claims should preserve; finance — [36], as above.

**2.3 Alternative operationalizations.** Six families have been tried:

1. **Closed psycholinguistic dictionaries** — LIWC's certainty/tentativeness categories [37] (the operationalization in [22]). Standardized and cheap; built for psychological states rather than epistemic warrant, and partly proprietary.
2. **Domain-built lexicons** — [36] in finance; [8]'s 25-word positive/negative set in biomedicine.
3. **Annotated corpora + supervised cue-and-scope detection** — the BioScope corpus [32] annotated speculation cues *and their scopes* in biomedical text; the CoNLL-2010 shared task [33] had ~20 systems compete on hedge and scope detection. The decisive advance over lexicon counting is scope: which material a hedge actually governs.
4. **Contextual certainty modeling** — [34] inferred statement strength from authors' own revisions; [35] measures sentence- and aspect-level certainty in science communication with trained models — the nearest modern neighbor to what Index C asks a scorer to do.
5. **Richer functional frameworks** — stance grammar [30]; the Appraisal engagement system [31]. More expressive than any word list; hard to mechanize, with historically poor inter-coder tractability at scale.
6. **Prescriptive calibrated vocabularies** — the IPCC's uncertainty language [38], with [39] showing readers systematically misread those phrases across languages and formats. [39] doubles as evidence for the mechanism Index C trades on: the mapping from epistemic marker to perceived certainty is real but noisy.

**2.4 State of consensus.** Split by community, informatively. In applied linguistics, Hyland's taxonomy and inventories are the de facto standard for transparent lexical analysis of academic stance — most cited, most replicated, the default baseline. The standing criticisms are equally established: polysemy and context-dependence (deontic vs. epistemic "may"; ability vs. possibility "can"), hedging being a *function* that word lists only approximate [29], non-lexical hedging (conditionals, attribution shields, agentless passives) escaping any list, and scope unhandled. In NLP, the consensus since [32] and [33] is that context-sensitive models beat lexicon lookup on detection accuracy, and the field moved accordingly (through [34, 35]). Net: consensus on the construct, and on Hyland as the standard descriptive inventory; **no consensus that lexicon counting is the best measurement** — it is the transparent, auditable end of a transparency–accuracy trade.

**2.5 Implications for this instrument.** The two-grain design (§1.1 above; codebook §4 vs. §8) straddles that trade deliberately: S1/S2 is the pinned-lexicon grain — mechanical, fully auditable; Index C is the context-and-scope grain — the weakest-marker chain rule (codebook §4.2) is precisely the scope problem [32] formalized, with the scorer doing the contextual work the NLP line automates, under quote-anchored audit. The lexicon critiques of §2.4 are therefore arguments *for* the redundancy, and for Index S remaining exploratory rather than confirmatory in directionality (§1.3's mathiness note [19] is the same caution from the other side).

**2.6 Proposals for the Appendix C.1 sign-off (pending; not enacted).** (i) Pin the Hyland inventory by exact source, edition and page — the article-form lists in [25] are the verifiable citation anchor; the monograph [4] is the fuller statement but has no machine-verifiable record of the cited edition. (ii) Record LIWC certainty/tentativeness [37] and the Loughran–McDonald strong/weak-modal lists [36] as robustness lexicons for S1/S2, sensitivity analysis only, never primary. (iii) Note [35]-style contextual certainty models as the available non-Hyland, non-LLM triangulation should an r22-adjacent validity check want one.
